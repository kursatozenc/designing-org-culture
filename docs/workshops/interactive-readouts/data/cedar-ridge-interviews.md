# Cedar Ridge Health — onboarding interview excerpts

Eighteen interviews, 45–60 minutes each, with people hired in the last 18 months,
plus four with managers who onboard. Excerpts below are the passages the research
team coded as load-bearing. Speakers are identified by role family and tenure
only. Quotes are lightly trimmed for length; nothing is paraphrased.

Use these the way you would use your own interview data: as the thing that
explains *why* a number moves, not as decoration under a chart.

---

## On the formal program

**P03 · Nursing · 7 months**
> Orientation was genuinely good. Three days, well organized, someone had
> clearly thought about it. I could tell you the badge policy, the escalation
> chain, where to file an incident report. Then I walked onto the unit and had
> no idea when it was okay to interrupt a charge nurse. Nobody covers that.

**P11 · Admin & Ops · 12 months**
> I rated onboarding high on the survey. I want to be clear that I meant the
> *program* was high. My first month was still terrible.

**P07 · IT & Data · 4 months**
> The handbook is 140 pages and it is accurate. That is the problem. Everything
> in it is true and none of it is what I needed on day four.

## On the person who told you how it actually works

**P02 · Nursing · 9 months**
> Marisol. That is the whole answer. She was on my first three shifts and she
> would just narrate — "we don't page him before eight," "that form is dead,
> everyone uses the other one." Two weeks in I felt like I worked here. My
> friend started the same month on a different unit and took until March.

**P15 · Allied Health · 6 months**
> There was no one. I am not saying people were unkind, they were busy. I
> learned by getting it wrong in front of someone and watching their face.

**P09 · Physicians · 14 months**
> Attendings are assumed to arrive fully formed. You get credentialing and a
> parking code. The social layer you are expected to already have, which is a
> strange assumption to make about someone who has never been in the building.

**P18 · Support Services · 3 months**
> My shift starts at ten at night. Everyone who could have shown me anything
> had gone home nine hours earlier. The night has its own rules and nobody has
> ever written them down, so you inherit them from whoever is willing to talk.

**P06 · Admin & Ops · 11 months · hybrid**
> On my in-office days I learn things. On my home days I do my tasks. I have
> been here almost a year and I still only know how it works on Tuesdays.

## On the AI assistant

**P13 · Admin & Ops · 5 months**
> Ridge Assist is great, honestly. PTO policy, which form, who signs it —
> instant. It saved me a lot of small embarrassment.

**P13 · Admin & Ops · 5 months** *(later in the same interview)*
> Ask me who I'd go to if I really messed something up. …Yeah. I don't know. I
> think I used up all my questions on the bot.

**P05 · Allied Health · 4 months**
> I would rather ask it than ask a person, because it doesn't cost anything to
> ask it a stupid question. Which I know is the wrong instinct. But it is the
> instinct.

**P16 · Nursing · 3 months**
> It answers the questions that have answers. Most of what I needed to know
> didn't have an answer, it had an *opinion*, and it had to come from someone
> who had been here.

## From the managers

**M02 · Nursing manager · onboards 8–10/year**
> We used to have a buddy system and it was informal, so when we got busy it
> was the first thing to go. Nobody decided to stop doing it.

**M04 · Support Services supervisor**
> Leadership looks at the orientation completion rate. It is at 97 percent. So
> from up there, onboarding is solved.

**M01 · Ops manager**
> Honest answer? I know in about week three whether someone is going to stay,
> and it has almost nothing to do with whether they finished the modules.

## One thing nobody told me — open text, selected

- "That the 7am huddle is optional in writing and mandatory in practice."
- "Which of the three shared drives is the real one."
- "That you're supposed to push back. I thought agreeing was the culture."
- "Where people actually eat lunch. I ate alone for a month."
- "That my manager prefers to be interrupted rather than emailed. Opposite of my last job."
- "Nobody told me it was okay to say I didn't know something."
